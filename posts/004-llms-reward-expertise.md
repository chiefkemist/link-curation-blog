# LLMs Reward Expertise

Sean Goedecke argues that the important prompting skill is not a bag of magic phrases; it is knowledge of the domain being discussed. LLMs make it easy for many people to produce a plausible first draft outside their specialty, but people with deep subject knowledge can steer the same model toward a more useful result.

Using Terence Tao’s mathematical conversation with ChatGPT as an example, the post highlights a pattern: concise prompts, direct attention to the important idea, willingness to push back on odd results, and specific questions grounded in the problem at hand. The broader software lesson is that familiarity with a codebase lets a developer challenge generic answers, recognize what is already present, and ask for a simpler or more appropriate design.

The post’s conclusion is deliberately human-centered: stronger models do not remove the need for expertise because someone still has to communicate what a good solution means and check whether the result fits the real system.
